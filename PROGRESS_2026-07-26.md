## 1. Local Fourier patches (`ExptsWithGlobalFourier/LocalFourierPatches.jl`)

The first few 2D DFT coefficients of a P×P patch (P = 5…40) at any location, on the
project's 224 padded field.

- **A windowed low-order coefficient *is* a Gabor response.** With a Hann or Gaussian
  window, `F(v,u)` is the inner product of the image with a windowed complex sinusoid.
  The sliding-patch maps behave accordingly: on a `T`, `|F(0,2)|` lights the vertical
  stem, `|F(2,0)|` the horizontal crossbar. This notebook and `CreateGaborLifting.jl`
  compute the same thing from opposite ends.
- **Energy fraction is the wrong thing to watch** — and this reversed a claim I had
  written before measuring. AC energy captured by orders ≤ 3 *rises* with patch size:
  **78 / 74 / 82 / 92 / 93 / 94 / 96 %** at P = 5…40, because a bigger patch of a
  blurred letter is *relatively* smoother. What degrades is absolute resolution
  `λ = P/K`: at P = 40, K = 3 the finest describable detail is 13 px — one whole
  stroke width. Useful band: **1–2 stroke widths**.
- **Polarity invariance is not free.** Under `I → 1−I`, `F → W − F` with `W` the
  window's own transform. Exact for a box window; for Hann/Gaussian at P = 21,
  `|W|`/DC is **0.54 / 0.34** at order ±1 but only **0.019 / 0.011** at ±2. First-order
  coefficients of a windowed patch are substantially polarity-*dependent*.

## 2. The tic-tac-toe Fourier signature (`TicTacToeFourierSignature.jl`)

Tile the 112 letter with a 3×3 grid; describe each cell by moments of its low-order
power spectrum (ink `a₀`, structure `ac`, orientation tensor `E₂`, `E₄`, ring
profile `e₁..₃`).

- **Orientation works, very well.** A bar swept through 12 angles is read to
  **0.16°** with polar sampling. The square DFT lattice is biased toward 0/45/90/135°
  by up to **3.48°** — its own 4-fold symmetry leaking into the 2nd harmonic.
- **`|E₄|` does *not* detect crossings**: single bar 0.18–0.23, plus **0.085**, X
  0.142. A single stroke rings the 4th harmonic *more* than a crossing does.
- **Loop vs blob needs the cell matched to the loop.** Disc vs ring of equal outer
  size gives `e₂/e₁` = 0.015 vs 1.33, but the ratio confounds hollowness with
  object-size-relative-to-cell; on real EMNIST at 3×3 it does not pick out `O`.
- **Diagnosticity** (12 classes, LOO nearest-class-mean, chance 8.3 %): ink alone
  56.7 %, **orientation alone (2 numbers/cell) 75.3 %**, ink+orientation 76.1 %, all
  nine features 74.2 %, and the same nine features with **no grid** 49.7 %. *The grid
  is doing the work.*

## 3. Global Zernike moments (`ExptsWithZernike/ZernikeCharacterMoments.jl`)

Zernike polynomials are orthogonal on a disc, indexed by angular order `m` and radial
order `n`; rotation maps `A_nm → A_nm e^{−imα}`, so `|A_nm|` is rotation-invariant and
the phase carries orientation.

- **Rotation invariance is real**: `|A_nm|` drifts ≤ **1.9 %** over 0–180° on the
  inscribed disc (exactly 0 at 90°, a pixel-grid symmetry) while the complex moments
  move **77–170 %**.
- **Fitting the disc to the ink is the biggest lever**: 64.7 → **73.1 %** (Re/Im),
  53.1 → 65.8 % (`|A|`). An unfitted basis spends itself on empty corners.
- **…but the fit degrades invariance ~5×** (drift 1.0–10.2 %), because the radius is
  re-estimated from each rotated copy. Accuracy and invariance are in tension.
- **Rotation invariance costs accuracy**: dropping the phase gives 73.1 → 65.8 %.
  Best is keeping both: **76.4 %** (75 numbers).

## 4. Zernike on the 3×3 grid (`TicTacToeZernike.jl`)

Better features, worse classification — and **not for the reason I first wrote down**.

- **Orientation to 0.24°** from `−arg(A₂₂)/2`; **`f₄` detects crossings** where the
  Fourier `|E₄|` failed (plus 0.699, X 0.689 vs bar 0.187); **loops read off the sign
  of `A₄₀`** (rings +0.75/+0.81, disc −0.29, bar −0.45), robust to ring thickness where
  the Fourier ratio flipped. On EMNIST, `O`-vs-rest AUC **0.839** at 1×1, in the right
  direction.
- **Yet it classifies at only ~66 %**, against 76.1 % for the Fourier grid.
- **The obvious explanation was wrong.** I attributed it to Zernike's hard disc
  boundary versus the Fourier Gaussian window. Measured: giving the *Fourier*
  descriptor a hard disc window costs essentially nothing (74.7 → 73.9 %).
- **The actual cause is a position/orientation confound.** Zernike moments are taken
  *about the disc centre*, so position leaks into shape: an **isotropic blob** — no
  orientation at all — slid off-centre reports its **position angle** as an orientation
  (30°→31.9°, 60°→58.1°, 90°→90.0°, 120°→121.9°). The Fourier cell descriptor is immune
  because it is built from `|F|²`, which discards phase. Across 749 inked EMNIST cells
  the two orientation estimates differ by a **median 43.3°** (45° = chance) despite
  agreeing to <1° on a clean centred bar.
- This retroactively explains §3's disc-fitting result: fitting **centres the object**,
  which is exactly the condition Zernike needs, and a grid cell cannot provide it.

**Transferable:** tiling space wants a *translation-invariant* cell descriptor;
describing one centred object wants a *rotation-invariant* one. Using either for the
other's job costs about 10 points.

## 5. Concatenating Zernike + Fourier (`CombinedZernikeFourier.jl`)

| descriptor | numbers | LOO | η²-weighted |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Z — global Zernike | 75 | 76.4 % | 77.5 % |
| F — Fourier 3×3 | 81 | 74.2 % | 77.2 % |
| **Z + F** | 156 | **82.8 %** | **84.2 %** |

Controls: shuffling F's rows (same columns, correspondence destroyed) gives
69.2–71.7 %, *below* Z alone — so the gain is complementarity, not dimensionality.
Error overlap: both correct 65.3 %, only Z 11.1 %, only F 8.9 %, neither 14.7 %.

**A wording correction recorded here**: I had called the 85.3 % "best-of-two" figure an
*oracle ceiling*. It is a bound on **selecting between** the two blocks, not on
concatenation — a joint feature space can classify an image neither block gets right,
and measurably does (6 of the 53 such images, 10 with η² weighting). Renamed throughout
to **best-of-two selection bound**.

## 6. All 47 classes, and merging identical shapes (`AllClassesDiagnosticity.jl`)

Every number in the project until now used the same 12 uppercase letters. On the full
47-class set (chance 2.13 %):

| label set | classes | Z | F | **Z + F** |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| strict | 47 | 55.5 / 57.4 % | 58.2 / 60.4 % | **62.5 / 65.3 %** |
| **homoglyphs merged** | **40** | 59.4 / 62.0 % | 62.7 / 65.2 % | **67.8 / 70.6 %** |
| homoglyphs + case merged | 31 | 57.4 / 59.4 % | 61.7 / 63.6 % | 66.9 / 67.7 % |

- **The ranking of the two blocks flips**: Zernike leads on 12 uppercase letters, the
  Fourier grid leads on all 47. Spatial layout scales with class count better than
  global shape does.
- **Merge groups must be disjoint by construction.** The obvious implementation — list
  confusable pairs, take the transitive closure — is wrong: `6≡G`, `G≡g`, `9≡g` chains
  `6` to `9` and collapses `6/9/G/Q/g/q` into one class. The notebook now asserts
  disjointness.
- **Merging case pairs makes things worse** despite fewer classes (70.6 → 67.7 %
  weighted). EMNIST-Balanced *already* merged the case pairs that look alike; the 11 it
  keeps are exactly those whose shape differs, so merging them creates **bimodal
  classes** a nearest-class-mean cannot represent (within-class scatter, merged ÷ parts:
  `D/d` 1.28 … `F/f` 1.04).

## 7. An MLP on the official split (`TestFeaturesWithMLP/`)

Everything above used **leave-one-out nearest-class-mean** — a deliberately weak
classifier chosen to make the *features* the subject. This section replaces it with a
plain fully-connected MLP trained the way EMNIST normally is: official **112,800 train
/ 18,800 test**, 47 classes, Adam, 15 epochs. A self-contained write-up defining every
term from scratch is in `README_MLP_FPE_Experiment.md`.

| input | numbers | test |
|:--|--:|--:|
| **156 hand features** | 156 | **86.43 %** |
| raw 784 pixels | 784 | 83.65 % |
| block F alone | 81 | 85.86 % |
| block Z alone | 75 | 83.96 % |

- **The features beat raw pixels by 2.8 points** at a fifth the size — a genuine
  re-description, not a compression.
- **Depth does not help**: 1×256 85.87, 1×512 85.56, 2×512 85.29, 3×512 85.03. The
  features have already done the nonlinear work.

**Three FPE codings** (this was the point of the exercise):

- **Concatenated FPE** (each scalar → its own code) **hurts monotonically** in both code
  size and bandwidth; best cell 85.58 %, the one least like an FPE code. It is random
  Fourier features, which help when a model *cannot* learn a nonlinear expansion — an
  MLP's first layer already is one.
- **Bundled FPE** (`V = 1/√N Σ_j R_j ⊙ z_j^{x_j}`, fixed width for any feature count)
  costs only **0.6 points**: 85.79 % at D = 1024 against 86.43 % raw. For a
  representation whose entire purpose is algebraic composability, that is a bargain.
- **Unbinding fidelity** (median R² recovering the 156 scalars analytically) is
  **negative below `D ≈ N = 156`**, exactly as `SNR ≈ √(D/N)` predicts, and reaches 0.94
  only at D = 4096.

**The result worth keeping:** at D = 256 the individual values are **unrecoverable**
(R² = −0.31, worse than guessing the mean) yet classification is **85.63 %** — within a
point of clean features. Decode R² sweeps from negative to 0.75 while accuracy moves
0.16 points. **`√(D/N)` is the right sizing rule for retrieval and the wrong one for
classification**: sized for readback you want D ≈ 4096; sized for a discriminative
readout D = 256 does the same job.

## 8. Why everything stops at ~86 % (`CeilingDiagnostics.jl`)

Every arm lands between 82 % and 86.5 % whatever the representation, and 16 conv
kernels is only a couple of points behind 32. Three diagnostics; the answer is mostly
**the dataset**.

| model | strict | homoglyphs merged | gain | share of errors |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| **156 hand features** | **86.43 %** | **92.53 %** | +6.10 | 45 % |
| small CNN (2 conv, stride 1, pooling) | 86.46 % | 92.70 % | +6.24 | 46 % |
| conv 11×11 s6 ×32, learned | 84.56 % | 90.80 % | +6.23 | 40 % |
| pixels 784, no conv | 83.65 % | 89.91 % | +6.26 | 38 % |
| conv ×64, frozen random | 80.48 % | 86.71 % | +6.23 | 32 % |
| conv ×32, frozen random | 78.62 % | 84.88 % | +6.27 | 29 % |
| conv ×16, frozen random | 73.19 % | 79.55 % | +6.36 | 24 % |
| conv ×8, frozen random | 63.44 % | 69.82 % | +6.38 | 17 % |

- **Nearly half the best model's error is undecidable labels** — merging `0`/`O`,
  `1`/`I`/`L`, `2`/`Z`, `5`/`S`, `9`/`g`/`q` lifts 86.43 → **92.53 %**.
- **The homoglyph error is the same absolute size for every model**: **+6.10 to +6.38**
  across models spanning **63 % to 86 %**, four architectures, learned and frozen. A
  model at 63 % makes as many `0`-vs-`O` mistakes as one at 86 %. Every model fails on
  the *same images*. That is what produces the clustering.
- **The convolution really is learning** — contradicting my own prediction. I expected
  freezing the kernels at random init to cost little (random-features argument) and to
  explain why the filters look unstructured. Measured: learned-32 **84.56 %** vs
  frozen-random-32 **78.62 %**, and frozen-random is still behind at 64 kernels. Worth
  ~6 points. The filters are unstructured *to the eye* but functional; they never look
  like edge detectors because stride 6 gives only 9 positions, there is no pooling, and
  no second conv layer needs clean oriented edges as *its* input.
- **A proper CNN buys ~1.3 points, not the ~5 I guessed**: 87.76 % best epoch, 86.46 %
  final — statistically identical to the hand features at the final epoch. So the
  plateau is **not** an artefact of avoiding convolution.

Of the ~13.6 % error the best model makes, **~6.2 points are undecidable** and only
~7.4 are addressable; within that margin four very different representations span
barely 3 points.

## 9. The methodological finding — read this one

**Leave-one-out nearest-class-mean does not merely understate accuracy; it can invert
qualitative conclusions.**

- It **understated the features by 24 points**: the same 156 numbers score 62.5 % under
  LOO-NCM and **86.4 %** through a one-hidden-layer MLP.
- Worse, it **manufactured a finding we had already written up**. Under LOO-NCM,
  concatenating Zernike with the Fourier grid was worth **+6.4 points**, and §5 above
  records that as strong complementarity with a shuffled-block control to back it. Under
  the MLP, **block F alone gets 85.86 % and Z+F gets 86.43 %** — 0.6 points, at the edge
  of significance. The complementarity was largely the weak classifier's inability to
  use F on its own, not a property of the representations.

The shuffled-block control was real and correctly executed; it established that the gain
was not dimensionality. It could not establish that the gain was not *classifier
weakness*, because that possibility was never in the hypothesis space. `KeyPointDiagnosticity.md`
had already flagged LOO-NCM as "a *floor* on the information" — the error was treating a
floor as a measurement when comparing *architectures* rather than features.

**Practical rules going forward:**

1. Use LOO-NCM for cheap feature triage only. Any conclusion about whether two
   representations are *complementary*, or which is *better*, needs a classifier with
   enough capacity to exploit each one alone.
2. On EMNIST-Balanced, quote the **homoglyph-merged** number alongside the strict one,
   or a descriptor gets blamed for an ambiguity that is in the task.
3. For comparing representations, this dataset is close to exhausted. ~6.2 of ~13.6
   points of error are undecidable, and the addressable margin is only ~7 points wide.

## Corrections made in this period

Recorded because the repository now contains both the original claim and its
correction, and the corrections are in the commit history:

| claim | status |
|:--|:--|
| Low-order energy fraction *falls* with patch size | **reversed** — it rises; resolution `λ = P/K` is what degrades |
| "Oracle ceiling" bounds the concatenation | **wrong** — it bounds *selection*; concatenation escapes it, measurably |
| Zernike-on-a-grid fails because of the hard disc edge | **wrong** — hard-disc Fourier costs nothing; it is a position/orientation confound |
| Homoglyph groups can be built by transitive closure | **wrong** — chains `6` to `9`; groups must be disjoint by construction |
| `F`/`f` confusion is label ambiguity | **wrong** — EMNIST keeps those shapes apart deliberately; it is a real descriptor failure |
| Frozen-random conv kernels would cost little | **wrong** — worth ~6 points; the layer genuinely learns |
| A proper CNN would reach ~91 % here | **wrong** — 87.8 % at best epoch on this budget |
| Z+F complementarity is +6.4 points | **artefact of LOO-NCM** — ~0.6 under an MLP |
