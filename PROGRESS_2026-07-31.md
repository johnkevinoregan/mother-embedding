# mother-embedding — progress, 2026-07-31

Continues from `PROGRESS_2026-07-30.md`.

That report ended with Phase 11: a frozen ImageNet ConvNeXt beat our 31 hand-designed features on
every property, and beat them *more linearly explicitly*, which retired one of the project's
central claims. What survived was narrower — an exact polarity invariance that 1.28 M photographs
could not buy.

**This period closes most of that gap.** Two changes to the front end, arrived at from opposite
directions, take it from 31 features to 53 and from a 0.24 deficit on thickness and fuzziness to

## 1. A fine scale at λ = 8 px

The ladder was 56 / 29.9 / 16 px against strokes 3–12 px wide — **the finest filter was wider than
most of the strokes it was measuring**. It could report that a stroke was present and roughly how
much energy it carried, but not resolve the stroke's own edge.

That is precisely what separates a **thick sharp** stroke from a **thin blurred** one: both put
energy in the coarse channels, and only the fine channels tell them apart. With nothing below
λ = 16 px the two mapped to overlapping feature values — a *degeneracy*, distinct causes producing
the same measurement — and the readout papered over it with a rule calibrated on the training
range. Hence a decent 0.71 on the ordinary test and **−2.06** once trained on sharp edges and
tested on blurred ones: not missing information, but a rule confidently applied outside its range.

λ = 8 (σ_x = 4.5 px) is the first channel small enough to sit inside a stroke. **Thickness +0.145,
fuzziness +0.148.** It is also where the ladder's own geometric spacing points — ratio 1.87 per
step puts the next rung at 8.55 px.

λ = 4 was also tried: it adds nothing further on brokenness (+0.002 after +0.041) though it keeps
helping fuzziness (+0.034). Below λ = 8 there is little left to see, since the generator's sharpest
edge is a 0.8 px ramp.

**This does not transfer blindly.** These stimuli are natively 112 px. EMNIST and Fashion-MNIST are
28×28 upsampled 4×, so λ = 8 sits at their original Nyquist limit and would mostly measure
interpolation.

## 2. A spatial max — and a bug two years old

**Every spatial statistic in the front end was a weighted mean.** No max, no quantile, nothing
sparse, anywhere.

A mean of an energy-like map is roughly **`signal strength × ink coverage`**. Two images with
identical local structure but different amounts of stroke give different means. So every pooled
feature silently multiplied "how strong is the structure here" by "how much stroke is in the
frame" — and thickness correlates strongly with coverage, so the features reporting thickness were
reporting a mixture the readout could not separate.

A max reports **peak local structure regardless of coverage**. Nine numbers — max of `A₁`, `A₂` and
ray `c₀` per scale — with **no new filters and no extra extraction cost**: thickness +0.113,
fuzziness +0.115, and small gains on every other row. About 78 % of what the extra scale bought,
for free.

**The project had already found this bug and misfiled it.** Phase 9 discovered the ray ratios were
formed per pixel and averaged, so the pooled value scaled with ink coverage, and fixed it with
pool-then-divide — recording it as a property of *bounded ratios*. It was never about ratios. It
affects every mean-pooled feature, and went unseen because there was nothing to compare a mean
against.

**Why a max and not a finer grid.** A grid also localises, but makes the representation
translation-variant, and position is randomised here — which is why grids 3 and 5 score *worse*
than grid 1. A spatial max is local **and** translation invariant.

It also explains how frozen ConvNeXt copes with global average pooling: its late units are sparse
and selective, so their spatial mean already behaves like a detector. `A₁` and `A₂` are dense
energy maps whose mean is dominated by everything that is not the feature of interest.

---

## 3. Why they add

On brokenness the combination is almost exactly the sum of its parts — `0.723 + 0.041 + 0.020 =
0.784` against **0.785** measured. The fine scale **adds information** that did not previously
exist in the bank; the spatial max **stops discarding information** that was already there. One
extends what is measured, the other extracts it faithfully, and there is no reason for them to
overlap.

## Also this period

**Readout architecture is not the limit, tested three ways.** Heads of 31→256→256→8, 31→512→128→64→8
and a residual 31→256→128→64 ⊕ 31 → 95 → 8, spanning 50 k–91 k parameters, all land within ~0.03 of
each other. Where the features are weak, no head recovers it.

**Predicted-vs-true scatters** for every property, arm and epoch, with binned medians and IQR —
`figures_predictions/`. They separate failures that R² conflates: the CNN is *compressed* toward
the mean (a bias), our features were *unbiased but imprecise* (a resolution limit), and they show
the CNN's non-convergence directly, its cloud drifting rather than tightening between epochs.

**A₁'s i1D floor** is now subtracted by default (`a1_floor=:analytic`), after `Validate_i1D.jl`
measured 4.6 × 10⁻² leakage at ρ=2 from the ±45° channel pair. Measured effect on published
tables: **≤ 0.006** — I had warned in three documents that it would invalidate them, and
overstated the risk.

**A CUDA front end** with `Validate_GPU.jl` gating it against the CPU reference (energy to 1.4e-5,
zero pixels differing by >5 % of RMS). 100× on the FFTs; 4.6× end to end, because host-side pooling
is now the bottleneck — a consequence of deliberately keeping `assemble` unchanged.

## Corrections

* **"The information genuinely isn't in our features."** Written after three head architectures
  changed nothing. The first half — that the readout is not the limit — stands. But it slid into a
  claim about the *front end* when it was only a claim about those 31 numbers, and one extra
  measurement recovered most of it.
* **Ruling out pooling.** I eliminated the pooling hypothesis because finer grids score worse on
  brokenness. That test changes two things at once — localisation and translation variance — and
  the second dominates here, so it could not isolate the first. The spatial-max experiment, which
  produced the second-largest gain of the period, exists because that objection was raised.
* **Cross-scale conjunction (`A₃`)** was reported as moving the thickness/fuzziness degeneracy by
  ~+0.16 in both directions. That was an inline reimplementation missing the `sqrt` that `assemble`
  applies to every A-block. Through the real path the effect is +0.019 and −0.270. Retracted.
* **Reduced-n selection inflates gains 2–5×** and reversed one sign outright. Use it to choose what
  to confirm, never for effect sizes.

## Still open

1. **Brokenness.** The one row nothing has moved. λ is exhausted (+0.041 then +0.002), the max
   gives +0.020, finer grids hurt. The live candidate is **σ_along**, the filter's reach *along*
   the contour, which fell only 4.7× while λ fell 14× because `n_orient` rises with `ρ`. Free test:
   `nori = [8,12,16,8]` — same wavelengths, same σφ, same feature count, σ_along at the finest
   scale 9.7 → 2.4 px.
2. **More ways of summarising a map.** The front end has exactly one — a spatial average — and
   every property is read through it whether it suits them or not. `P9_P12_SimpleStrokeTests/RESULTS.md`
   sets these out in full; in brief, in the order I would try them:
   * **coverage-normalised averages**, `mean(A₂)/mean(C₀)` — "corner strength per unit ink".
     Removes the structure × coverage confound explicitly rather than dodging it, and keeps the
     averaging, so it is far steadier than a max. The machinery already exists: it is the
     divide-after-averaging fix Phase 9 applied to the ray ratios and never extended to `A₁`/`A₂`.
   * **unevenness** (spatial variance) — an unbroken stroke gives a smooth stroke-end map, a broken
     one gives the same map plus two isolated spikes. Huge in the variance, small in both the mean
     and the max. Best candidate for brokenness.
   * **counting above a threshold** — "how many places respond strongly". Arm count *is* a count,
     and brokenness is about whether two extra stroke-ends exist. Neither an average nor a maximum
     can express "there are two of these". Never tried, and cheap.
   * **a high percentile rather than the literal maximum** — one odd pixel currently sets the max;
     the 95th percentile is nearly as sensitive and much harder to fool. Worth running as a
     robustness check on the max results already obtained.
   * **max on the remaining blocks** — `C₀` and `lowpass` are trivial. The ray *ratios* need the
     value at the location of maximum `c₀`, not the max of the ratio, which would find the emptiest
     patch of background. The orientation harmonics have no per-pixel map at all, because
     `assemble` averages before forming them — the same ordering the conjunction layer exists to
     avoid, and never compared against the alternative.
5. **Re-run the changed-conditions splits** with the 53-feature configuration. The λ=8 arm alone
   improved the degenerate rows there (+0.64, +0.92) but *cost* 0.09–0.27 on the shape rows, because
   fine channels go silent on blurred images. Whether the spatial max offsets that is unknown, and
   it decides whether this configuration should become the default.
6. **Phase 6 re-verification** and **BSDS**, both carried over.
