# mother-embedding — progress summary, 2026-07-30

Continues from `PROGRESS_2026-07-26.md`.

That report ended with the Fourier grid, Zernike moments, and the MLP re-evaluation that
overturned the leave-one-out conclusions. This period builds the thing those experiments were
pointing at — **`RationalGaborFeatures/`**, a bio-inspired early-vision front end — and then
spends most of its effort discovering that the standard way of testing it cannot answer the
question it was built to answer.

The numbered **phases** start here; `PHASES.md` at the repository root is the map. Three new
directories:

- **`RationalGaborFeatures/`** — the front end, and Phases 0–8 on EMNIST.
- **`SimpleStrokeTests/`** — Phase 9. A synthetic dataset with **graded properties** instead
  of class labels, which is where the change of method happened.
- **`FashionMNIST/`** — Phase 10. The first dataset in this project that is not a line
  drawing.

**The arc, in one paragraph.** The front end is validated against synthetic ground truth and
reproduces a known EMNIST number exactly, gaining **+1.35 points** from the bank alone. But
the conjunction layer — the whole theoretical payload — adds **≈ 0** on EMNIST, across five
independent lines of evidence. An attempt to build a synthetic task where it *must* help
failed three times. The resolution was to stop asking *can a classifier separate these* and
start asking *how much of each property is linearly available*, which is Phase 9 — and under
that question the conjunction layer pays **+0.16 to +0.21**, the front end beats a properly
trained CNN on four of five geometric properties, and a bug that had made it **not polarity
invariant** came to light. Phase 10 then removes the last objection to that result — on
Fashion-MNIST, a dataset this project did not construct, the same layer is worth **+1.62 to
+2.85 points** against a shuffle control.

## 1. The front end (Phases 0–4)

Every parameter fixed by measurement rather than convention. The scale ladder comes from
EMNIST's measured spectrum — ρ = 2.00 / 3.74 / 7.00, so λ = 56 / 30 / 16 px at 112 — against
an earlier hard-coded set that put one channel past Nyquist and another where ~1 % of the
energy lives.

Six validator notebooks gate it: exact quadrature, zero DC, RMS normalisation, polarity
invariance, the junction ordering straight < L < T < X, that `theta` is the carrier everywhere
(checked to 1.0° on a full 2π ray-direction recovery), and pooling with a `shuffle_block!`
control built in from the start.

**Bugs found and fixed while building it**, each of which had a visible tell:

| symptom | cause |
|:--|:--|
| adding padding made results *worse* | ρ in cycles-per-field, not cycles-per-image — every filter mistuned 2.86× |
| 63 % feature shift on padding | `sum(G²)` normalisation scaling as 1/√(HW); fixed with RMS |
| filters longer than any stroke | `dtheta_on_sigma = 1.5` gave σ_along = 34 px; 0.75 fixed it and dropped the field 320 → 224 |
| A₂ collapsing to plain energy | absolute-ε conditioning; fixed with the relative `κ·E(x)` |
| A₂ weak at line ends | max over orientations, 2.5×; the *dominant* orientation gives 10.4× |

## 2. Phases 5–8 — the conjunction layer does not help on EMNIST

**Phase 5a.** Reference arm reproduces the previously published 92.31 % exactly. The new bank
gives **93.66 %** — **+1.35 points** from the ladder, more orientations and correct padding.
The AND layer adds **+0.06, then −0.06**.

**Phase 5b.** Is 3×3 pooling destroying a point property? Partly — A₁+A₂ alone gain +3.1 points
at 6×6 — but the *baseline* does not improve either, so the task is saturated at 3×3. A fact
about EMNIST, not about conjunctions.

**Phase 5c.** Flat at every sample size from 200 to 112,800 images. The shuffled twin earns its
keep: at 200 images, 54 *shuffled* columns cost −10.10 points, so `+A` costing −0.44 is itself
evidence those columns are informative.

**Phase 6 — the cleanest positive result in the project.** Augmentation buys a small CNN
**+3.9 to +12.8 points** and buys the features **−1.2 to +0.4**. That is direct evidence the
features carry the invariances, which is a much stronger claim than "features win by 7.7".

**Phase 7 explains 5a–5c.** `R²(A ← orient) = 0.933` median across 40 classes. Different
operators, near-collinear *on handwriting*. And `F`/`f` is near-undecidable — pixels 66.4 %,
CNN 67.5 %, CNN with augmentation 69.9 %, our features 69.9 %.

**Phase 8 — three failures, and the failures are the finding.** Three synthetic designs meant
to require co-location; everything solved all three, down to a 2 px gap against a 9–15 px
stroke. **Any co-location difference also changes local ink density**, and a classifier finds
that instead. It also exposed a conflation: Phase 3's cosine 0.97 means two feature vectors are
*close*, not that a trained classifier cannot separate them.

## 3. Phase 9 — changing the question (`SimpleStrokeTests/`)

Phases 5–8 all asked *can a classifier separate these*, and on synthetic stimuli the answer is
nearly always yes. Phase 9 asks instead: **fit a linear readout and measure how much of each
property it recovers.** A property that needs a hidden layer to extract is present in the
representation but has not been made *available* by it, and making it available is what a front
end is for.

A new dataset: one stroke on a uniform grey field, described by **eight graded properties** —
curvedness, brokenness, closedness, vertex angle, arm count, thickness, edge softness, polarity.
Five arms, three extrapolation splits, everything scored against a trivial three-scalar
baseline rather than against zero.

**Results.** A linear readout on our features beats a two-hidden-layer MLP on 12,544 raw pixels
on every structural property, and a properly trained CNN on four of five. **The conjunction
layer finally pays** — +0.16 to +0.21 over a shuffle control whose spread across five
permutations is ≤ 0.002; on EMNIST the same layer was worth +0.01. Trained on light strokes and
tested on dark, our numbers are unchanged while both pixel arms fall below chance and the CNN
reaches −2.1 on corner angle.

**The best configuration is 31 globally pooled features with a small MLP** — 0.925 / 0.737 /
0.938 / 0.954 on the structural rows, against the CNN's 0.411 / 0.298 / 0.290 / 0.859.

### Three front-end bugs this dataset exposed

**Polarity invariance was broken.** `orient` predicted contrast polarity at R² 0.65 when
quadrature energy should predict nothing; features differed by 29 % between a stroke and its
exact contrast-reverse. The bank zero-pads, and on a **non-zero background** that puts a
full-contrast step round the border, whose cross term flips sign with polarity. Fixed by
padding with `:replicate`; invariance is now exact to 2.4 × 10⁻⁷. **EMNIST could never have
shown this, because its background is zero.**

**Ray ratios were formed per pixel and then averaged**, guarded by an absolute epsilon writing
0 where energy vanished — which asserts "perfectly symmetric" at locations with no evidence,
and makes the pooled value scale with ink coverage. Now the numerator and denominator are
pooled separately and divided afterwards. This one is conceptual as much as numerical: the
guard encoded the assumption that images have empty regions, true of line drawings and false
of photographs.

**And a rule I stated too broadly.** "No absolute epsilon anywhere" is wrong — the real test is
whether the fill value is the quantity's *limit*. It is for `A₁` and `A₂`, which are
energy-weighted and tend to zero; it is not for a bounded ratio. The distinction is
**energy-like versus scale-free**, and it also explains why only the ray ratios needed the
pooling change.

## 4. Phase 10 — Fashion-MNIST (`FashionMNIST/`)

The first dataset here that is not a line drawing: filled silhouettes with **texture**, which
multi-scale oriented energy had never been tested on. It also brings **published baselines** —
the replicating cluster is CNN 93.7–94.6 %, ViT 92.6–93.6 %, linear on pixels 85.2 % — so the
result is calibrated externally.

**Results.** Features reach **89.70 %** from 198 numbers, against 87.70 % for an MLP on 12,544
raw pixels and 93.10 % for a CNN trained here at native 28×28 under the same protocol. The
pixel arm matching the published ≈ 88 % is what validates the harness. A₁+A₂ alone reach
86.51 % from 54 numbers.

**The headline is that the conjunction layer pays on a dataset this project did not construct.**
Against a 5-permutation shuffle control, A₁+A₂ are worth **+1.62 at grid 3 and +2.85 at grid 1**,
σ ≤ 0.27, where EMNIST gave +0.01. The control is doing real work: read naively against the base
the layer looks worth +0.67, but the 54 extra columns are themselves harmful (−0.95 shuffled,
against EMNIST's independently measured −0.75 for the same block), so the naive reading
understates conjunction by 2.5× and would have confirmed the prediction that it adds ≈ 0. This
is the strongest form of the Phase 9 finding, because the objection that the task was built to
suit the operator does not apply.

Of three predictions recorded beforehand, 1 and 3 held and 2 was wrong. **Grid 3 beat grid 1 by
8.0 points**, settling the Phase 9 grid result as being about *position randomisation* rather
than about pooling. The CNN winning by 3.4 points is the mirror image of Phase 9, where it lost
on four of five geometric properties — geometry is what the front end is for, texture at 28×28
is what a learned bank is for, and the inductive-bias argument predicts both.

The **ray block does not earn its columns here** — +0.54 over the base, −0.04 on top of A₁+A₂,
for 81 features. Expected on silhouettes with no junctions, and support for the standing
complaint that the ray harmonics cost more than they return.

A Pluto notebook shows every stage as dense heatmaps on a chosen garment.

## Corrections made in this period

Listed because several were claims this project had already published.

- **"No task has been constructed on which the conjunction layer beats the orientation
  statistics."** Superseded by Phase 9. The EMNIST conclusion stands; it was a statement about
  the **data**, not the operators.
- **The CNN comparison was against a handicapped opponent.** Two strided convolutions, 12 CPU
  epochs, validation still rising. Rebuilt full-resolution with 60 GPU epochs: `arms` went
  0.650 → 0.859 and the claim weakened from "beats it on all five" to four of five.
- **`|c₁|/c₀` is not dead weight.** Reported as contributing ≤ 0.015; that was an artefact of
  the per-pixel ratio, and it is +0.021 once pooled correctly.
- **"A₁+A₂ own corner angle, rays own arm count."** Half survives — rays own `arms` (+0.113
  against +0.022), but `vangle` is a tie once the pooling is fixed.
- **`closedness` is confounded** and should not be read as a closure result: open and closed
  arclengths do not overlap, so a length threshold classifies every image correctly. Geometric,
  not a coding error — a closed contour can be π·D long where an open arc capped at 2π/3 of turn
  is limited to ~1.2·D.
- **The Phase 5a published numbers do not reproduce** within 0.06. The reference arm is
  bit-exact and two re-runs agree to four significant figures, and no code change accounts for
  it, so the original run's conditions were not captured in the repository.
- **The CNN has ~0.03 run-to-run variance**, so describing `arms` as "a dead heat at 0.842
  against 0.843" was reading a decimal that does not exist.
- **Design verdicts from Phase 9 are dataset-specific.** Divisive normalisation "losing" was
  measured on stimuli with one stroke width and near-constant contrast — the conditions under
  which it would help are absent. `RESULTS.md` now carries that caution over the whole file.

## Still to do

1. **`R²(A ← orient)` on Fashion-MNIST.** Phase 7 explained the EMNIST null with 0.933 — A and
   orientation energy near-collinear *on handwriting*. If that is the explanation, the same
   regression on garments must come out markedly lower, and the two numbers together would
   account for the whole +0.01 → +1.62 difference. The features are cached; this is minutes.
2. **An i1D response gate on `A₁`.** Zetzsche & Barth (Vision Research, 1990) prove no linear
   filter can be i2D-selective and give the criterion — the quadratic kernel must vanish on
   collinear frequency pairs. So `A₁` should read ≈ 0 on a straight line at *every* orientation,
   and will not be exactly 0, because orientation channels have bandwidth. If it leaks, `A₁` is
   partly a function of orientation energy by construction, and Phase 7's 0.933 is a fact about
   our implementation rather than about handwriting. Bears directly on item 1.
3. **Phase 6 has not been re-verified** against the current front end. It is provably
   unaffected — it uses no rays and padding is bit-identical on EMNIST — but Phase 5a already
   falsified "provably unaffected" once. It is the expensive one, ~3 h, because augmentation
   multiplies the feature extraction tenfold.
4. **BSDS boundary detection.** The real target. Natural greyscale images have spatially
   varying contrast, several scales at one location, multiple objects and no empty background —
   every condition our datasets lack, and every condition under which divisive normalisation
   and the pooling grid would actually be adjudicated.
5. **`dtheta` is still a fitted constant**, and `:structure` anchoring for `d` has never been
   exercised by an experiment. It is a switch, not a result.
6. **A stimulus with mixed stroke widths.** The ray transform is **not thickness-invariant** —
   a 4 px bar crossing a 15 px bar reads as two rays, not four — and neither existing dataset
   can reveal it, since both hold stroke width constant within an image.
