# Thoughts

Short notes that are **not experiments**: identities, relations between things the project already
has, and arguments about what a result means. Each should be readable on its own.

The distinction from a phase directory is that nothing here introduces a measurement to justify a
design decision. If a note turns into an experiment, it moves out and becomes a phase.

| note | what it says |
|:--|:--|
| [`FourierGaborBankRelations.md`](FourierGaborBankRelations.md) | The orientation block is a polar-binned local power spectrum — provably the same numbers a plain FFT of the cell would give. So it is *not* where this front end differs from the Fourier baseline it was built to beat; the pointwise nonlinear layer above it is. Also: what the higher orientation harmonics measure — they are junction-*angle* detectors (`Z₄` for 90°, `Z₆` for 60°, mutually exclusive), and they were tested on a dataset with no 60° junctions. And why the scale axis has no counterpart to the orientation axis' size/shape split — which is what the thickness/fuzziness degeneracy is. |
| [`CompareWithGrueningBarth.md`](CompareWithGrueningBarth.md) | Grüning & Barth's Min-Nets start from the *same* Zetzsche & Barth theorem we do — no linear filter is i2D-selective, so you need an AND of two filters — and then **learn** the pairs inside a deep net where we **fix** them at 90°. What differs (phase-sensitive signed responses vs. our quadrature energy; AND-of-ANDs vs. one pass at the image; they never test any invariance) and the verification they omit (nobody checks the learned pairs are orthogonal or oriented). Two things worth stealing: **POCP** as a robustness metric that cancels baseline accuracy, and **hyperselectivity** as a formal property of the operator rather than of the stimulus set. Also the route their thesis does not consider: the **ray transform** is *linear* in the energy field, so the AND buys the energy field but not junction type — and conversely their min keeps the spatial layout that makes A₁ provably unable to count rays. |
| [`ResidueHDCExplained.md`](ResidueHDCExplained.md) | **Written to be read cold** — assumes no knowledge of hyperdimensional computing, residue number systems or resonator networks, and builds each from nothing. What Kymn et al. actually did and why it works: why random vectors in high dimensions are nearly perpendicular, the two operations (binding = componentwise multiply = *add the angles*; bundling = superpose), FPE as "let `D` clocks run for time `x`" so that adding numbers becomes multiplying vectors, the exact condition for the code to wrap, the Chinese remainder theorem as three gears of 3, 5 and 7 teeth, and the resonator network as a search that keeps every candidate alive at once instead of enumerating combinations. Ends with a glossary and a note on which sections of the paper to read. |
| [`CompareWithKymnResidueHDC.md`](CompareWithKymnResidueHDC.md) | The Redwood group's residue hyperdimensional computing is built on **the same FPE** `P0.3_New_Gabor_FPE/` uses — their Definition 4 is our "Bug A" in a different parameterisation — and their scene equation is our architecture with convolutional sparse coding where our front end would go. What they have that we do not is the **read side**: a resonator network that factorises a bundle back into identity and position, so pose is *recovered* rather than discarded. Also: why their new multiplication operator does **not** rescue FPE for the early AND layer (integers only, and rationals break commutativity), what residue moduli and hexagonal coordinates would buy our position encoding, and the experiment — swap our front end into their equation 5.5 and measure resonator convergence, a figure of merit with no labels in it. |
| [`CompareWithOsarioShapeMatters.md`](CompareWithOsarioShapeMatters.md) | Osório, Bernardino & Wichert reach the same Attneave keypoints by a **third route** — trace the boundary, simplify the polyline, keep the corners — which sidesteps Zetzsche & Barth entirely by not being a filter, at the price of needing a cleanly thresholdable figure. The substantive point is that the two invariances are **split between us**: their object-centred polar `(r, θ)` gives the translation and scale invariance our fixed `g × g` grid does not have, while their raw-patch descriptor loses the polarity invariance their own contour stage already has. Suggests two cheap experiments in both directions. Also: **ETL1** as a real out-of-distribution test set for Phase 13, why their Table 1 is a weak-baseline comparison and Table 2 is not, and that "no training" hides seven hand-set thresholds. |
| [`ExploitingFourierHarmonics.md`](ExploitingFourierHarmonics.md) | **Written to be read cold** — assumes nothing about this project or about Fourier analysis. Why the front end cannot tell "two strokes meet" from "two strokes are both nearby", why no calculation on its summaries can fix that, and that the missing ingredient is **phase**, which we discard in the first line of the first function. With the measurements: keeping phase separates the two cases 3.87× against 2.18× for strength alone — but our existing detector still manages 9.74×. |
